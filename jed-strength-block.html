Your resting burn is about 2,464 kcal (Mifflin–St Jeor). With four lifting sessions, three cardio sessions and 12,000 steps a day, maintenance lands near 3,600 kcal. Eating 2,350 leaves a deficit of roughly 1,250 a day — about 1.15 kg of fat a week, or 5 kg a month. Every figure on that list is an estimate with about 10 % of slack in it. The seven-day weight average is the real instrument, and Progress tells you exactly what to move when it disagrees.

Straight talk

What 5 kg a month costs you

Why it's achievable

At 136.7 kg you have a large fat store to draw energy from, which is exactly the condition under which a big deficit spares muscle rather than eating it. High protein and heavy lifting are the other two conditions, and both are built into this plan. Around 1 % of bodyweight per week is the usual upper bound for someone at your size; 1.15 kg is 0.85 %, so you are inside it, not past it.

What it costs

Muscle gain gets slower. In a deficit this size you should expect strength to climb steadily — more weight on the bar most weeks — while visible new muscle stays modest. That is a fair trade: you will finish twelve weeks meaningfully stronger and about 15 kg lighter, with the muscle you already carry intact and far more visible. The serious size-building phase works much better once you are leaner, and this is how you get there fastest.

The floor that isn't negotiable

Do not chase the number below 2,100 kcal or under 210 g of protein. Past that point you stop buying fat loss and start buying muscle loss, hunger you can't manage, and sessions where the weights go backwards. If a month comes up short, buy the difference with steps, not with less food.

Structure

The week

Four lifting days on an upper/lower split, three cardio days, no fully idle day — Sunday is a walk and a cook-up. Lower and upper alternate so nothing gets trained two days running. If life moves a day, keep the order; never do two lower days back to back.

Sessions

The four sessions

Barbells and machines throughout. Every session opens the same way: 5 minutes on a bike or a brisk incline walk, then 10 slow reps each of bodyweight hip hinges, arm circles and ankle rocks, then two light ramp-up sets of the first exercise before your working sets.

The first exercise of each day is the one that matters — go in fresh, rest properly, and add weight there before anywhere else. Everything after it is supporting volume.

How to add weight

Double progression. Take every set to the top of its rep range — 4 × 8 on the squat, say — then add the smallest increment the gym has and start again at the bottom of the range. Weeks 1–2, stop each set with about 3 reps left in the tank while you learn the machines and find your loads. From week 3, 1–2 reps left. In a deficit this size, adding weight most weeks on the four opening lifts is the win.

Deload weeks

Week 6 and week 12: same exercises, same weights, half the sets, skip the carries. You come back stronger, and it keeps the knees, elbows and lower back intact across a long block. Do not skip these — on 2,350 kcal you are recovering with less than you would on maintenance.

About your 15 kg dumbbells

Be honest about what they can do. At 136.7 kg, a 15 kg pair is too light to progress your legs, chest, back or shoulder press — you'd be doing 30+ rep sets on a goblet squat long before the muscle was challenged, which is a cardio session in disguise. They stay useful for exactly three things: Bulgarian split squats (where they'll be plenty for a month or two), lateral raises, and curls and rear-delt work.

If you miss a gym day entirely, do this at home instead of nothing: 3 rounds of — 15 Bulgarian split squats per leg, 20 push-ups (feet elevated if easy), 15 dumbbell rows per side, 20 lateral raises, 45 s plank. It is a holding pattern, not a substitute. Get the four gym sessions.

Conditioning

Cardio and steps

This is where the 5 kg a month actually comes from. The food target alone gets you to roughly 3.5 kg; the walking closes the gap. Steps are the lever you reach for first whenever the scale runs slow — they cost you almost nothing in recovery, which is not true of eating less or training more.

Nutrition

Eating

Four day-templates. Rotate them A → B → C → D and start again; every one lands within 20 kcal of target and clears 205 g of protein. No ugu, no efo, no okra, no ewedu — the vegetable side is built from cucumber, lettuce, cabbage, green beans, tomato, tatashe and carrot, all raw-friendly, none of them slimy or bitter.

Swaps that keep the maths intact

  • Chicken breast, turkey breast and gizzard trade one-for-one by weight.
  • 100 g of sardines ≈ 90 g of minced beef.
  • 150 g cooked rice ≈ 210 g boiled Irish potato ≈ 200 g boiled sweet potato ≈ 3 slices bread.
  • 1 egg ≈ 30 g mozzarella.
  • Cucumber, lettuce, cabbage, tomato, tatashe and carrot all swap freely by weight. Green beans are about twice the calories of the others, so trade 100 g of green beans for 200 g of anything else.
  • Still hungry? Cucumber and lettuce, without limit. 200 g is 30 kcal.

The one gap this vegetable list leaves

Cucumber and lettuce are excellent for volume and almost nothing else — they are largely water. Dropping the dark leafy greens costs you folate, iron, vitamin K and magnesium that ugu and efo would have supplied. The carrot, tatashe and tomato cover vitamin A and C, and the sardines bring omega-3, vitamin D and calcium, but the gap is real.

So the daily multivitamin moves from sensible to necessary. Any cheap complete one will do. If you ever decide to try one leafy green, green amaranth is the mildest of them and would close most of this on its own.

Feedback loop

Holding the 5 kg

Weigh yourself every morning — after the toilet, before food, same clothes. Ignore any single reading; a plate of rice and a hot night can move you 1.5 kg overnight. What you act on is the seven-day average against the week before. You need that number to average 1.15 kg or more.

The three that decide it

  • Protein every day. Miss the calories occasionally; never miss the 210 g.
  • 12,000 steps every day. Not an average across the week — a daily floor.
  • 7–9 hours of sleep. Short sleep raises hunger, blunts recovery, and pushes the weight you lose away from fat and toward muscle. It is the highest-leverage free thing on this page.
Before you start

Worth getting checked

See a doctor for a baseline — more so at this rate

At 136.7 kg and 195.6 cm your BMI is about 35.7, and you are about to run a large deficit for twelve weeks. One appointment before you start: blood pressure, fasting glucose or HbA1c, and a lipid panel. It may change nothing, and if it does show something you want to know now rather than in week nine. Mention loud snoring or daytime sleepiness if either applies — sleep apnoea is common at this weight and treating it makes everything else easier.

Two risks that come with fast loss specifically

  • Gallstones. Losing more than about 1.5 kg a week raises the risk, and very low fat intake raises it further. This is why fat is held near 75 g a day rather than cut to the floor, and why 1.6 kg/week is a signal to eat more. Report persistent pain under the right ribs.
  • Dehydration and cramps. Twelve thousand steps in Lagos heat on 4 L of water is the plan, not a suggestion. Salt your food normally — you are sweating it out.

Stop and get advice if

  • Chest pain, pressure or unusual breathlessness during effort
  • Dizziness or fainting
  • Joint pain that persists after the session rather than fading — especially knees or lower back
  • Numbness or shooting pain down a leg or arm
